# Leveling Rubric v3 — Competency Axes

The rubric scores candidates along five axes, each on a 1-5 scale. Final level is determined by the *minimum* score across axes, not the average — a candidate strong in three axes and weak in two does not get the higher level.

## Axes

### 1. Technical Scope
- 1: Implements assigned tasks
- 2: Owns features within a project
- 3: Owns projects within a team (L3)
- 4: Owns systems within a team (L4)
- 5: Owns systems across multiple teams (L5+)

### 2. Technical Depth
- 3: Recognized strong within team
- 4: Recognized strong across adjacent teams
- 5: Recognized strong across the engineering org (L5+)

### 3. Autonomy & Ambiguity
- 3: Self-directs within defined projects
- 4: Defines projects given problem statements
- 5: Identifies and frames problems before they're assigned (L5+)

### 4. Influence & Leadership
- 3: Mentors junior engineers on their team
- 4: Drives technical alignment within their team
- 5: Drives technical alignment across teams (L5+)

### 5. Communication
- 3: Writes clear design docs reviewed by peers
- 4: Writes design docs adopted by other teams
- 5: Writes documents that set technical direction for the org (L5+)

## Hiring Calibration

A candidate must score 5 on at least 4 of the 5 axes to be hired at Staff. A 4 on Communication is sometimes acceptable for Staff if the rest are strong; a 4 on Technical Scope is not — scope is the defining axis for Staff.

The rubric is the source of truth for the debrief. Interviewers must rate against the rubric, not against their general impression. "Strong candidate" is not a rating; "5 on Technical Depth, 4 on Influence" is.
